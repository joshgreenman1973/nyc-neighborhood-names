When a neighborhood got its name

Of these 38 neighborhood names, 19 took off only after 1950, beginning with Cobble Hill, which first appeared in the Times in 1960.

The map and chart follow 38 New York City neighborhood names through 152,247 Times article matches and mark when each name first appeared. Of those names, 19 are new names that took off after 1950, seven were pushed and never caught on, seven have been in steady use since before 1950 and five faded and came back.

Counts are the number of Times articles that match a search for the name in quotation marks plus the name of its borough. They are raw counts: the Times published a different number of articles each decade, and not every match uses the name for the neighborhood. The search reports at most 10,000 matches per period, so pre-1950 figures shown as 10,000+ (Harlem, Long Island City, Flatbush and Coney Island) are floors. Gray cells come before the name's first use and are other meanings, like London's Soho; the map ignores them. The first use is the oldest article whose headline or summary shows the name used for the place, read by hand. It is the first appearance in the Times, which can be later than the first use anywhere. Full details are in the methodology.

Everything substantive (the idea, the choice of names, the sources and how to read them) was decided by the author, a human. The coding and the file work were done mostly by Anthropic's Claude and Claude Code. The author questioned Claude closely throughout and insisted on quality checks. For this project those were: every first use was chosen by reading the headlines and summaries of the oldest matching articles one by one and rejecting the ones that used the words some other way; two names were dropped when their searches could not be separated from ordinary words; every count is the API's own hit total for a query published in the methodology; and the map and chart were checked for overlapping labels at desktop and phone widths. The risks of using AI remain, and we would be grateful to hear of any errors you spot.
